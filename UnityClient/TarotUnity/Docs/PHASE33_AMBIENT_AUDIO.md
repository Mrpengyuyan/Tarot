# Phase 33 Ambient Audio

Date: 2026-06-16

## Purpose

The game shipped with a fully wired audio system but no sound: `AudioManager` and
the seven presentation cues (MenuOpen, SpreadSelected, ShuffleStarted, CardDealt,
CardFlipped, ResultReady, ResultReveal) were triggered during play, but `cueMap`
was empty, so every cue fell back to a crude single-sine beep, and there was no
background music. This phase gives the game an actual warm, calm "Moonlit Tarot"
sound: seven interaction SFX plus a looping ambient music bed.

## Why Synthesized (Not Downloaded)

The audio is generated from scratch (pure-Python synthesis, no numpy), so it is
**100% original and royalty-free** - there is no third-party license to track,
which is the safest option for a downloadable game. Downloading clips of uncertain
license and processing them was the alternative, but original synthesis is more
reliable here and licensing-clean. If you later want specific real recordings, drop
the WAVs into `Assets/Audio/` and re-point the `AudioManager.cueMap` bindings - the
system takes any AudioClip.

## What Was Added

- `Assets/Audio/SFX/` - seven mono WAVs, one per cue. Bells/chimes for tonal cues
  (additive inharmonic partials with decay envelopes), filtered-noise riffle for
  shuffle, a soft transient + woody thud for dealing, a rising-lowpass whoosh for
  the flip, an anticipatory pad swell for "result ready", and a bell cascade +
  sparkle for the reveal. Light multi-tap reverb for space; peaks normalized below
  0 dBFS (no clipping).
- `Assets/Audio/Music/MUS_AmbientMoonlit.wav` - a 24s loopable A-minor drone pad
  (detuned sine pairs for warmth, a slow breathing LFO over exactly two loop
  cycles, a gentle high shimmer, and a short equal-power head/tail crossfade for a
  seamless loop).
- `AudioManager` gained an `ambientMusic` clip + `musicVolume` and auto-plays the
  bed (looping) on Awake, so music persists across scenes via the existing
  `DontDestroyOnLoad` service.

## Wiring

`Phase33AudioBootstrapper` imports the WAVs (SFX = decompress-on-load PCM; music =
streamed Vorbis) and assigns them on the persistent `AudioManager` in `Boot.unity`:
the seven cues get clips with balanced per-cue volumes, and the ambient bed is set.
Because `AudioManager` lives on the same GameObject as `GameBootstrap` (found via
`EnsureService`), these serialized clips are the instance that runs at play time.
The procedural-beep fallback is left enabled as a safety net.

## How To Run

Editor menu: `Tools/Tarot Unity/Run Phase 33 Audio Bootstrap`. The audio itself is
regenerated by `gen_tarot_audio.py` (kept under `Docs/Audio/`); tweak its
parameters to re-voice any sound.

## Exit Criteria

- The eight WAVs import as valid AudioClips; the Boot `AudioManager` binds all seven
  cues to non-null clips with sane volumes and has an ambient music bed.
- EditMode and PlayMode tests pass.

## Remaining Limitation

Phase 33 gives the game a cohesive baseline sound; it is not final high-end VFX or a
composed soundtrack. Exact timbres, mix balance, and whether music should duck or
change per scene are taste calls best tuned from a live listen - all cue volumes,
the music volume, and the synthesis parameters are easy to adjust.
